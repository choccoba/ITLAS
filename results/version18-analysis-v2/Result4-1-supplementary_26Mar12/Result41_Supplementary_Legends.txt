Figure 6. Disease Trajectory: IT→IA Transition and CR Scar. (A) TGFB1 in blood NK cells across disease spectrum, showing IA-emergent upregulation (NL→IT NS; IT→IA ★↑43.9%, p=0.016, 20/20 consistency). Supplementary Figure S4. TOX Lineage-Specific IT→IA Transition Pattern in Liver. (A) TOX donor-level expression in liver myeloid cells across disease groups (NL, IT, IA, AR, CR). Red circles represent individual liver donors; horizontal bars indicate group means. TOX showed a striking IT-specific spike in myeloid cells (NL→IT ★↑1857%, p=0.004, 36/36 consistency) followed by near-complete elimination at IA (IT→IA ★↓96.1%, p=0.007, 30/30). IA expression (mean=0.0033) fell below the NL baseline (mean=0.0044), representing a true transition pattern. (B) TOX donor-level expression in liver CD4+ T cells across the same disease groups. In contrast to myeloid cells, TOX in CD4+ T cells showed chronic-persistent upregulation: NL→IT ★↑101% (p=0.009) with IT→IA non-significant (p=0.931), indicating that TOX elevation persists through the IA phase in the T cell lineage. This lineage-specific dissociation — IT-specific in myeloid cells versus chronic-persistent in CD4+ T cells — demonstrates that the IT→IA transition involves selective correction of aberrant myeloid programs while T cell exhaustion programming remains intact. Compare with Figure 4A (TOX CD4+ T liver-specific upregulation). Mann-Whitney U test; ★p<0.05. Panels: 2 (A, B)

Supplementary Figure S5. GZMK IT→IA Transition in Liver Myeloid and CD8+ T Cells. (A) GZMK donor-level expression in liver myeloid cells across disease groups. Similar to TOX, GZMK showed aberrant IT-phase expression in myeloid cells that was nearly completely eliminated upon transition to IA (IT→IA ★↓98.4%, p=0.012, 29/30 consistency). GZMK expression in myeloid cells is atypical and suggests an aberrant cytotoxic program acquired during the IT phase. (B) GZMK donor-level expression in liver CD8+ T cells. Unlike the myeloid pattern, CD8+ T cells showed significant IT→IA downregulation (IT→IA ★↓22.7%, p=0.030, 27/30), consistent with reduced cytotoxic gene expression upon immune activation. Red circles: individual liver donors; horizontal bars: group means. Mann-Whitney U test; ★p<0.05. Panels: 2 (A, B)

Supplementary Figure S6. Blood NK Cell Immunoregulatory Phenotype Acquisition at IT→IA Transition. Donor-level expression of three IA-emergent genes in blood NK cells across disease groups: (A) TGFB1 (IT→IA ★↑43.9%, p=0.016, 20/20; NL→IT NS), (B) SERPINE1 (IT→IA ★↑341.4%, p=0.016, 20/20; NL→IT NS), (C) ID3 (IT→IA ★↑770.3%, p=0.032, 19/20; NL→IT NS). All three genes were non-significant at NL→IT and became significant only at the IT→IA transition (IA-emergent pattern), suggesting that blood NK cells progressively acquire an immunoregulatory phenotype during disease progression from IT to IA. TGFB1 and SERPINE1 (PAI-1) are associated with TGF-β-mediated immunosuppression, while ID3 is a transcriptional repressor of effector differentiation. Blue triangles: individual blood donors; horizontal bars: group means. Mann-Whitney U test; ★p<0.05. Panels: 3 (A–C)

Supplementary Table S3. Comprehensive IT→IA Transition Gene Analysis (Tissue-Separated). Donor-level Mann-Whitney U tests for 24 transition-relevant genes across 4 lineages (Myeloid, CD4+ T, CD8+ T, NK) in both liver and blood. Three comparisons are shown for each gene-lineage-tissue combination: NL→IT, IT→IA, and NL→IA, enabling pattern classification. Patterns defined as: TRANSITION (NL→IT significant AND IT→IA significant with opposite direction), IT-persistent (NL→IT significant, IT→IA non-significant), IA-emergent (NL→IT non-significant, IT→IA significant), Chronic (NL→IA significant only). Only combinations with at least one significant comparison (p<0.05) are included. Genes tested: TGFB1, ID3, SERPINE1, TOX, GZMK, SNAI1, SOCS1, SOCS3, LAYN, TOX2, LGALS9, AIM2, MEFV, DNMT1, DNMT3A, TET2, JAK1, MTOR, PRDM1, FOXP3, GNLY, TIGIT, CTLA4, BCL6. Statistical test: Mann-Whitney U, two-sided, donor-level means. ★p<0.05, †p<0.10.
